# CattleClass age and weight implementation plan

**Date** — 21 November 2026

**Status** — CID benchmarks completed. Weight passed the CID numerical gate and age missed its gate. The local field workflow has passed synthetic verification. Collection and independent field validation remain open. [Measured results and run commands](traits.md)

Add estimated age and live weight alongside the existing five-breed classifier. Keep a single RGB photo as the first input to test. Use Python 3.12, TensorFlow's Keras API, EfficientNet-B0, and the existing Gradio app. Start with an isolated experiment on CID, then decide separately for age and weight which estimates can be shown to users.

## 1. What the existing data supports

I joined the original CID CSV to the checked-in fold manifest using the original SKU in each retained photo's path. The audit counts an original SKU once and keeps the existing duplicate-linked group as the split unit. The checked-in manifest contains the published breed splits. The current processed index contains breed and fold information. Age and weight are available in the raw metadata. [CID publication](https://doi.org/10.1145/3542954.3543018) · [Upstream dataset](https://github.com/bhuiyanmobasshir94/CID) · [CattleClass fold manifest](../results/cid_folds.csv)

| Evidence | Observed in the retained CID sample |
|---|---|
| Sample size | 1,893 photos, 503 original SKU records and 371 duplicate-linked groups |
| Recorded age | 375 at 24 months, 123 at 30 months, and 5 at 36 months |
| Recorded weight | 150 to 698 kg, with a median of 239 kg |
| Sex in the metadata | 499 bulls and 4 heifers |
| Label disagreement within duplicate-linked groups | 25 groups contain more than one age value. 47 contain more than one weight value. |
| Sparsity at the upper age label | The five held-out folds contain 0, 1, 2, 1, and 1 records at 36 months. |
| Active dataset scope | CID supports the default studies. Specialist development and calibration can use separately sourced references for each task. The reserved final cohort needs all three linked references. |

In the 513 raw rows, the two-incisor label agrees with 24 months in all 381 records that report 24 months. Of the 126 records reporting 30 months, 125 list four incisors. All six records reporting 36 months list six incisors. This is strong evidence of association between the fields. The source materials reviewed do not establish how the individual ages or weights were measured. The upstream data-collection repository describes scraping cattle listings. Treat CID values as **recorded metadata** and seek confirmation of scale readings and how age was assigned before treating them as reference measurements. [Upstream collection repository](https://github.com/bhuiyanmobasshir94/Cow-weight-and-Breed-Prediction) · [Mississippi State Extension on estimating age using dentition](https://extension.msstate.edu/publications/estimating-cattle-age-using-dentition)

### Measured baselines to beat

Fit the weight medians on the other four outer folds and score one prediction per held-out original SKU. For the image-based breed baseline, use the saved CattleClass E2 fold predictions for the first retained photo in sorted path order. The CID cache index matched the published fold manifest and the saved predictions passed the training ID, fold and model checksum checks. Choose the predicted breed's weight median, with the global training median as a fallback for an unsupported breed. Use recorded breed only as a diagnostic.

| Weight baseline | MAE across 503 records |
|---|---:|
| Global training median | 44.10 kg |
| Training median for breed predicted from the photo | 39.33 kg |
| Training median for recorded breed | 34.52 kg |

For the age experiment, 498 records have one of the two supported labels. Always predicting 24 months gives 75.3% exact-band accuracy, 50.0% balanced accuracy, and 1.48 months MAE when the band labels are represented by their recorded values. That MAE measures error against the two CID labels. It does not measure accuracy against birth records. The five 36-month records are outside this benchmark. Repeat all baselines on the same eligible cases used by each candidate.

## 2. Approaches and recommended order

| Approach | What it tests | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| One RGB photo and small Keras heads | Learns cues from coat, body shape, and the rest of the visible animal. An ordinary photo can confound body size with camera distance and perspective. | Start here. Compare separate heads with a shared backbone before settling on the simpler model that meets the benchmarks. |
| Standardized side photo with a scale marker | Uses a full-body view taken with the animal standing side-on and a reference of known size in the same plane as the torso. Test geometric features such as height, body length, and silhouette area alongside the image model. | First fallback for **weight** if ordinary photos do not meet the usefulness threshold. Collect paired captures to measure the benefit. |
| Paired views or depth | A side and top or rear view can supply width. Depth can supply volume and physical scale under a defined capture protocol. | Evaluate later if a standardized side photo remains insufficient and collection conditions support a second view or sensor. |
| Lower-incisor photo and birth records | A specialist can use eruption and wear to estimate a broad age range. Reliable birth records and photo dates provide chronological age for validation. | First fallback for **age** if full-body performance is weak. Use images captured by trained handlers during routine checks and validate tooth-stage labels with an independent reviewer. |

Published work supports testing standardized views and depth. A dual-view RGB study used 3,210 paired images from 107 Simmental cattle under fixed acquisition conditions. A separate depth study used 196 Angus steers. Their reported errors apply to those datasets and setups. [Dual-view RGB study](https://doi.org/10.3390/ani16162532) · [Depth study](https://doi.org/10.3389/fanim.2026.1752658)

**Recommended first version** — Run the CID weight benchmark first. In parallel, test whether a full-body photo distinguishes records labeled 24 and 30 months. Treat the 36-month cases as descriptive and leave them out of binary training and primary scoring. A public age estimate needs birth-date-based validation across the intended ages. The first product scope is one RGB photo, with separate approval for age and weight. A calibrated side photo can still use one upload. A second view or a dental photo would be a separate mode.

## 3. Data contract and split rules

- Derive `original_sku` from the photo's parent folder using the same whitespace normalization as CID indexing. Use the processed index's `animal` value as `group_id` and preserve its test `fold`. The raw SKU provides the label. The group controls the split. Keep the original labels when similarity links distinct records and their identities remain uncertain. Quarantine any identical or confirmed same-session photo assigned contradictory labels for the affected target, using a written rule fixed before model fitting. Publish a sensitivity comparison excluding all groups with target disagreements, and report exclusions and changes in sample size.
- Join `age_in_year` as months and `weight_in_kg` as kilograms. Add `age_valid` and `weight_valid` independently. Missing, nonnumeric, nonfinite, or nonpositive targets fail validation for that target. Keep the other valid target. Store source validity and protocol eligibility separately, so a valid 36-month record remains in the audit and is excluded from binary age fitting and scoring. Stop on duplicate normalized SKU records with conflicting metadata. Report unusual but parseable values for review and document any exclusion rule before evaluation.
- Keep paths relative to the data directory and match the cached path order. Keep raw metadata, images, checkpoints, and per-animal predictions private. Track aggregate metrics and model provenance in Git.
- Use only image pixels as model input for the first experiment. Keep SKU, site, sex, breed labels, dentition, height, price, filename, and EXIF metadata out of the input tensor. Use metadata for analysis and splitting. Use original paths only to join records.
- Retain the five existing outer folds to compare against published breed performance. Select each candidate, preprocessing choice, epoch budget and target transform inside grouped inner validation. Check support for both age classes in every inner split. Lock the candidate-selection rule before producing outer-fold predictions. Fit the selected pipeline on the outer development data using the chosen settings, then score the held-out fold once. Use outer scores as development evidence. Any change prompted by those scores needs a fresh cohort for confirmation before release. Keep all views and repeat visits of each animal in one partition and review possible cross-collection duplicates.
- The primary benchmark evaluates one eligible photo per original SKU. For the unfiltered benchmark, pick the first retained photo in sorted path order, before seeing labels or predictions. For a defined side-photo mode, select the first view passing a documented quality review performed without access to labels or predictions. Report eligibility and coverage against the full submitted cohort before filtering. Check whether results depend on the first-view rule by repeating scoring across retained views with equal SKU weight. In a separate multi-view experiment, combine scores from views of the same animal and report the number of photos required.
- Use all views during training with sample weights that give each original SKU equal total weight within each head's valid subset. Track image, SKU, and group counts. Compare a geometry-preserving crop with the current center crop for weight, since cropping or normalization can remove silhouette and scale cues. Keep the input format fixed for the initial benchmark before introducing additional comparisons.

## 4. Model and evaluation design

**Baselines and shortcut checks.** Recompute the measured baselines on the eligible cases for each experiment. Fit all medians using outer development data, and use verified out-of-fold breed predictions on held-out photos. For weight, start with regularized linear regression on frozen EfficientNet embeddings. Run a small matched comparison with a crop restricted to the animal and a background-only control on a reviewed subset. Report whether the control still predicts the labels or performance collapses when the background is removed. Use that evidence to decide if more diverse, independently captured data are needed. If calibrated side photos proceed, compare image regression with a simple regression using measured silhouette features on the same animals. Add the same reference baselines in the field with medians from field development data.

**Keras models.** Start from ImageNet-initialized EfficientNet-B0. Train two single-task models, starting with a frozen backbone and global average pooling. Try staged fine-tuning only if selected by the inner validation protocol. The CID age head is a binary classifier for the recorded 24- and 30-month labels. Use cross-entropy, with class weighting as an inner-validation option. Score the predicted class, and compute the recorded-label MAE by mapping that class to 24 or 30. Do not average probabilities into a month estimate. Choose field age bands and record their birth-date-based definitions before training on the field dataset. A dental fallback uses its own capture and labels.

For weight, use a scalar output with Huber loss on standardized log kilograms. Fit scaling on the training subset and invert the transform before evaluation. Keep the regularized baseline if a neural head provides no material improvement. Share a backbone with breed only if independent models demonstrate value and one shared network measurably reduces deployment cost. For that optional comparison, keep the current breed loss and class order. Normalize each head's loss by its valid sample-weight sum using finite labels and independent masks. An all-missing head contributes zero loss. Test metrics and gradients with missing labels.

| Question | Metrics and comparisons |
|---|---|
| Can CID age labels be separated? | Pool held-out predictions. Report balanced accuracy, recall for each of the two classes, confusion matrix, support and recorded-label MAE. For the five 36-month cases, report which supported class the classifier assigns as a descriptive check. A two-class output cannot establish whether an unlabeled photo belongs to a supported age range. |
| Does the photo improve weight prediction? | Report MAE, RMSE, median absolute error, signed bias, and fraction within ±10% of recorded or verified weight. Show predicted versus reference weight and residuals by weight. |
| Who does the model work for? | Report support and error by site, sex, breed, age or weight range, and capture quality. Give both the number of unique animals and, where applicable, the number of duplicate-linked groups. Mark sparse strata as insufficient. |
| Does sharing hurt breed prediction? | Compare the shared candidate against the existing model using paired predictions, the same CID folds and photo eligibility. Report macro-F1 and per-breed recall. |
| How precise are the comparisons? | Report per-fold scores and paired bootstrap intervals for baseline improvements. Resample duplicate-linked groups in CID and animals in field evaluations. Report sites separately when site support is too limited to estimate between-site uncertainty. |

### Pilot decision rules

Lock the candidate protocol and these proposed thresholds before evaluation. Passing a CID gate permits the next experiment.

1. **Weight.** Require at least 10% lower held-out MAE than each of the global and predicted-breed baselines on the same cases. Require the lower bound of the paired 95% interval for the absolute MAE improvement to be above zero in both comparisons. On the audited cohort, the stronger baseline is **39.33 kg**, giving an illustrative gate of **35.39 kg**. Recompute the actual gate when the eligible cohort changes. The true-breed median is diagnostic because it uses the recorded label.
2. **Age.** Require pooled balanced accuracy of at least **0.55**, with its paired 95% improvement interval above zero relative to the constant baseline. Report per-class recall and recorded-label MAE. The age gate qualifies a follow-up study of age bands using documented birth dates. Keep the CID age output internal.
3. **Breed.** If testing a shared model, require a paired 95% lower bound of at least −0.02 for its macro-F1 difference from the current breed model on matched cases. Agree on an acceptable per-breed recall change before testing. Keep the separate breed model if the shared candidate misses either criterion.

### Field release rules

- Before accessing held-out reference labels, record the intended use, sites and populations, allowed capture modes, maximum errors and interval widths, required observed coverage and display rate, minimum subgroup support, and how each criterion is calculated. A threshold that depends on the intended use stays unset until the project has a defined use and field protocol. Keep the estimate off until those requirements are written and passed.
- Keep model development, calibration, and final site testing disjoint by animal. Predefine the image-quality rule during development and register the held-out media alongside the test CSV. Keep a private ledger of test animals to prevent reuse for a changed model or policy within the same trait and capture mode. Start with split conformal intervals for weight on absolute residuals within each capture mode, targeting 90% marginal coverage on comparable cases. Calibrate age as a set of supported bands on the field model and predefine when to withhold an overly broad set. Select interval-width and display rules in development, then lock the complete system before the reserved test. The observed interval coverage after filtering must be assessed separately. The original marginal coverage target does not certify coverage for an individual, for every subgroup, or after filtering.
- On the reserved site, report valid input rate, display rate out of **all** submitted attempts, coverage among displayed estimates with reference labels, and the fraction of all labeled attempts that both received an estimate and were covered. Report missing reference labels and rejection reasons. Evaluate safety and utility for the claimed cohort. If a locked system fails and is changed, use a fresh locked test cohort to assess the revision.
- Start field deployment with enrolled sites and capture modes. Confirm eligibility through the controlled workflow. A photo alone cannot reliably establish origin, age support, or all other cohort restrictions. An ordinary public upload can continue to receive breed scores. Age and weight require independently approved field artifacts and an eligible capture mode.

## 5. Field data plan

1. **Pilot the collection protocol on 30–50 independently identified cattle.** Use at least two participating farms or sites if feasible. Capture an ordinary phone photo, a standardized side view without a marker, and a standardized side view with a marker for the same animal and session. Use the middle condition to measure what standardizing the pose adds before attributing gains to physical scale. Record how often the animal is fully visible, the marker is in the correct plane, and a reference measurement is available. The pilot estimates collection quality and variance. It is not an accuracy certification.
2. **Record reference labels at the time of photography.** Record live kilograms from a calibrated livestock scale, equipment ID, date/time and any failed weighing. Keep weight and photos in the same session and document allowable timing and equipment checks before collection. Record an independently documented birth date and the photo date for chronological age. Use an approximate or unknown label flag where appropriate. A dental assessment can serve as a separate ordinal reference when birth records are unavailable. Keep those cases out of chronological-age validation.
3. **Capture protocol.** Use one standing animal, full torso and legs visible, minimal obstructions, and a straight side view with the camera approximately perpendicular to the animal. Place a known-size, high-contrast marker close to the torso plane and facing the camera with its corners visible. Before applying a scale correction, check the marker size and image detection, framing, camera angle and expected geometry. Document what an operator must verify in person, especially that the marker and animal are at similar depth. Keep the uncalibrated and calibrated views distinct. For a paired-view subset, add a consistent second view and keep both frames tied to the same animal and session. Record camera and site for analysis, with permission to retain the photos and anonymized records.
4. **Expand to the population the demo will serve.** Recruit across sexes, breeds, and age and weight ranges, including groups scarce in CID. Include different sites, phones, seasons, and reasonable lighting. Reserve at least one independently collected site for the final test. If only a later period at the same site is feasible, restrict the claim to that site's temporal test. Keep repeated animals within one split. Estimate required sample size from pilot error variance, desired precision of interval coverage and MAE, site clustering, and support needed per subgroup. A provisional collection budget is 300–500 unique animals across multiple sites, to be revised after the pilot.
5. **External data screening.** For any public candidate source, verify license and access, stable animal IDs, capture setup, image-to-label pairing, units, and how the relevant labels were established. Assess specialist sources for their intended task. For the joint final test, require all three references for a scored animal and keep its animals and sites separate from those used to develop or calibrate any of the models.

### Implementation decisions

The CID benchmark uses small, separate Keras and Ridge heads over frozen EfficientNet features. Weight's inner selection chose Ridge on all five folds, so the private final model wraps Ridge in a serializable Keras head. The weight background control was competitive on a small reviewed subset. On 95 independent CowDatabase2 cattle, the frozen weight model underestimated every published live-weight value from both side views. The next weight step needs independent field photos with verified scale references and varied backgrounds. The age fine-tuning check was confined to inner validation and lowered balanced accuracy. A shared model, marker-to-measurement geometry, and dental training remain gated by independent evidence. [CowDatabase2 check and limitations](traits.md#independent-weight-check-on-cowdatabase2)

The follow-up did not justify adopting tight crops or enabling breed rejection. CID supports only the existing exploratory age comparison because just five animals have labels beyond 24 and 30 months. Keep the paired field pilot and a multi-site reference dataset as the next validation milestones. [Capture and breed follow-up](traits.md#capture-and-breed-follow-up)

**First deliverable** — A repeatable CID benchmark that compares the new image heads with strong baselines, reports what the data can and cannot establish, and identifies whether an age or weight estimate merits field validation. Documented performance on CID remains development evidence. The reserved field test determines which estimates are suitable to display.
